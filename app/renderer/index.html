    Anatomy of a spread

    A spread is two facing pages. Three things make it different from an ordinary image.

    Trim
    The finished page size — where the blade cuts. 10800 × 3600 px here.
    Bleed
    38 px of extra image outside the trim, deliberately sacrificial. The cut drifts about a millimetre; without bleed that drift leaves a white sliver down the page edge. Client exports remove it; lab exports keep it.
    Safe area
    Keep faces and text inside this. Anything close to the trim risks being clipped.
    Fold
    The crease where the album is bound — 282 px wide. A face landing here is swallowed by the binding. The engine slides the crop, and punches in up to 18%, to walk subjects out of it.
    Slot
    A photo rectangle defined by the template, stored as fractions so one template works at any album size.

    What comes out

    Client proof
    One HTML file with every spread inside it. Email it — it opens on any phone, no internet needed. This is what you send the couple.
    PDF
    One spread per page at true album size. For review, or a lab that asks for PDF.
    PSD
    Layered Photoshop file with guides on the trim and fold, for a designer to finish by hand.
    Spread JPEGs
    Written automatically into the output folder at full resolution with bleed — what the printer wants.
